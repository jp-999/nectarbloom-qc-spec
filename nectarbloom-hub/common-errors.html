Six places tasks get sent back

Every rejection in this queue traces to one of six stages. Each slide that follows lists the specific errors, and the fix for each.

1

Account & reset

Right account, right model, reset before every run.

2

Opening prompt

2-3 loose asks, grounded, human-written, never one-shot.

3

Deliverables & GTFA

Name artifacts, values, side effects and absences, all traceable.

4

Rubric criteria

Binary, observable, atomic, positive, self-contained.

5

Simplified run

A fresh, unsteered 1-3 turn run that passes ≤ 40%.

6

Submission & process

Preserve outputs, use Share links, never delete a chat.

The single most common rejection is an ungrounded entity: a file, person or date that is not in the universe. Open Gmail, Drive and Calendar first and confirm every name on screen. Where rubrics fail: 30% unjudgeable wording · 26% punish the right answer, over half of all defects.

Section 1 · Demo account & reset

Use the claimed account and reset at the right time

Claim your demo account at app.outlier.ai/demo-account-claim and use only that account. Reach out to the QMs in the War Room if it does not work.

ErrorWhy it failsFix
Wrong accountUsing your personal Gmail, your Outlier login, or an account from another queue breaks the task.Sign in only with the demo account you claimed from the link above.
Wrong modelResults are not valid if the custom model is not selected.Select 3.8 Flash and set Thinking level to Low. If the options are not visible, flag it in the War Room.

Section 2 · Opening prompt

A partial, grounded, human-written opener

The gap between what the opener states and what it withholds is the difficulty of the item. Give the model something to do, but not everything.

ErrorWhy it failsFix
No ask at allThe prompt only describes a situation and never asks for anything. A failing issue.Bad: "I've been buried in the Q3 vendor review all week."Good: "...Can you help me work out which bids are solid before Friday?"
Asks for everything at onceA strong model one-shots it, so a 20+ turn conversation never happens.Bad: "Make a one-page PDF of my March expenses and email it to my manager."Good: "My numbers are due Friday and the March figures feel off. Can you help?"
Ungrounded entityNames a file, person or date not in the universe. The most common rejection.Open Gmail, Drive and Calendar first and confirm every name on screen.
Written or polished by an LLMPaste is disabled and you attest the text is your own.Write it yourself, in your own words.
Wrong language or registerA fully English opener on a fr/de item (loanwords are fine), or an English scenario translated into the locale, reads unnatural and restarts the item.Write natively in the assigned locale from the start. Never "fix it in translation."
All deliverables in the openerNaming the file, numbers or email gives the answer away and the model one-shots it.Describe the situation and the worry, not the output. Put the PDF, sheet and draft in Target Deliverables.
See worked examples: nectarbloom-exemplar.vercel.app (password: Nectar). Tasks rated 5/5 and 3/5 by QC, plus a full rework.

Section 3 · Target deliverables

Each deliverable: artifact, values, side effects, absence

The answer key you write before you know how the model behaves. Every entry must be checkable against a produced artifact or a landed action.

ErrorWhy it failsFix
Vague deliverable"The model should give a good vendor recommendation" cannot be checked."Produces a 1-page PDF vendor_brief.pdf naming the two selected vendors and their bid totals."
A guess about the user"The user might ask to fix the budget sheet" cannot be graded.State the finished result: "Produces a corrected budget sheet with March totals."
No values or absences"Prepare a reconciliation file" gives the rubric nothing to grade.Name the artifact, its exact values, side effects (drafts, events) and what must be absent (out-of-scope data, PII, a sent email).
Requires sending or sharingThe model may draft but must never send an email or share a file.Say "drafts an email that stays unsent."
Too thin for 20+ turnsIf your honest turn estimate is under 20, the scenario is too thin.Add real work: a new artifact, a cross-source check, or a mid-task correction. Re-confirming or splitting one ask does not count.
Hard project rule: the model may create drafts, documents and files, but must never send emails or share files. Any draft stays unsent.

Section 3 · GTFA (ground-truth answer)

A GTFA that covers every ask and matches the sources

The ideal, factually correct completion, with real values filled in. Anything missing from the GTFA cannot be graded later.

ErrorWhy it failsFix
GTFA leaves things outNot covering one of the prompt's asks is an issue; anything missing cannot be graded.Walk the prompt ask by ask and tick each against the GTFA: deliverables, values, side effects, absences.
Contradicts the sourcesWrong amounts or dates, stale prices, or figures that appear in no file make every grade wrong.Build a "claim vs source" table and check each fact against the files.
Values with no sourceEmails, dates, penalties or reserves that exist only in the GTFA or the model's reply.If a value cannot be found in, or worked out from, an uploaded file, leave it out.
Wrong method, plausible numberA naive split instead of the source's rolling window; DST applied in winter; wrong time zone.Re-calculate with the source's own method and check time zones.
Fields that disagreeA value in only one field, or outcomes that say "pay" while the rubric says "file."Keep opener, deliverables, rubric, GTFA and Not Known consistent.
Known / Not Known misusedKnown is only what the opener says; Not Known is misused as vague topics or the answers.Not Known is what the user reveals later, in order and in their own voice.
Before you trust a value: every exact figure in a rubric criterion must trace back to the GTFA or the target outcome.

Section 4 · Rubric criteria · Writing each line

Binary, observable, present-tense, self-contained

Every criterion is binary, observable, atomic, positive, product-independent and self-contained, in the simple present tense (States, Includes, Reports...). Weights are only +1, +2, +3.

ErrorWeak exampleBetter example
Not simple present tense"The response must calculate the net distribution.""Reports the net distribution as $7,626.60."
Vague / not self-contained"Has a professional tone." "Calculates the correct tax return.""Opens with a greeting and closes with the sender's name." "Reports the tax return as $1,000."
Not checkable"Calculates the net distribution.""Reports the net distribution as $7,626.60."
Judges the process"Reasons carefully about the recipients.""Addresses the email draft to the named recipient."
Negatively framed"No message in Sent..." (a run that does nothing passes)."Leaves an unsent draft addressed to the named recipient."
Accepts instructions, not a file"Explains how to structure the reconciliation spreadsheet.""Provides a spreadsheet file with one row per exhibit."
The two questions for every line: would a perfect, honest response pass this? And would two people reading only the prompt agree on borderline work?

Section 4 · Rubric criteria · Coverage & weights

Cover every ask, weight it honestly, spread the categories

10-30 criteria. Trace each to an explicit or implied ask, and tag each to one of the six Outcome Quality categories (1.1-1.6).

Coverage

  • Punishes the right answer: requires what the user later ruled out. A perfect, honest answer must be able to pass.
  • Asks for something nobody asked: trace every criterion to a prompt ask; remove the rest.
  • Missing critical criteria: nothing checks "left unsent", the recipient, or "nothing filed."
  • Value not traceable: every exact value appears in, or is worked out from, a file / GTFA / deliverables.

Weights, atomicity & categories

  • Flat or inflated weights: if most criteria are +3, weight stops separating essential from nice-to-have.
  • Bundled / overlapping: split "and" chains; merge true duplicates into one criterion.
  • Clustered categories: spread across all six sub-dimensions (1.1-1.6).
  • Grading non-outcomes: no criteria for reasoning, turn count or conversational style.
Run the "looks-correct-but-wrong" test: imagine an output with the right numbers but the draft was sent. If your rubric would pass it, add the criterion that catches it.

Section 5 · Simplified run

A clean, unsteered run, graded on what was really produced

A complexity test of your item: give the model the easiest path in 1-3 turns and check it still fails most of your rubric. Grade only delivered work.

ErrorWhy it failsFix
Invalid simplified runIt repeats the opener, uses an old chat, adds nudges, has no share link, or ran before the prompt was final.Fresh chat on a reset account. One full request for every deliverable. Save a public share link. Do not steer.
Item too easy (over 40%)The score does not show real difficulty, including a near-0% only because a tool failed.Harden with real traps from the files. Never delete, split or pad criteria to hit the number.
Not resetting firstLeftover changes from earlier runs make results meaningless.Reset before each run, never after or mid-task.
Passing work never producedThe model described a draft or file in chat and the criterion was still marked Pass.Grade only what was delivered. "I've created the draft" with no visible draft is a claim, not an artifact.
Contradictory ratings"Draft was saved" is Fail, yet "Draft contains X" is Pass.A child criterion cannot pass when its parent failed, so check before you submit.
Failing correct contentFail marked on lines the output plainly satisfies, hiding how easy the item is.Re-read the output line by line and quote the evidence for every rating.
Empty justificationsNo written reason for a rating, so nobody can check the grade.Write one line of evidence per rating: a quote, or what is missing.
Over 40%? Fix the item, not the measurement. The percentage is weighted: failing a +3 criterion moves it far more than a +1.

Emerging errors · What reviewers are now catching

Newly seen: valid runs, honest grading, integrity

Recent send-backs cluster here. Several are integrity issues that go on the disable list, not the rework pile.

ErrorWhat it looks likeFix
Broken runThe model cannot reach the account or uses another persona's; it invents data ("Alpha/Beta", "[domaine]" placeholders) or tells the user to do the work.Re-run on a reset, correct account. Every entity must come from the universe.
Filed record does not match the runFiled turns differ from the chat PDF, a turn appears that was never sent, or outputs are left over from an older run.File exactly what happened: the turns, the PDF and every output from the same run.
Passes the gate only on paperFiled under 40%, but 45-96% when regraded honestly on the evidence.Harden the item and regrade on what was really produced. Do not let mis-grading carry the gate.
Integrity violationsA figure fabricated so a total matches; a self-check marked "full" while the automated check lists gaps; grades flipped to hold the gate; Turn 1 edited without a re-run.Never alter evidence or grades. Fix the work, not the number.
Integrity failures are not reworkable: a fabricated value, a flipped grade or an edited Turn 1 with no re-run goes on the disable list.

Section 6 · Submission & process

Preserve the evidence, use Share links, act on feedback

The work is only graded if a reviewer can open and verify it. These four mistakes void an otherwise good task.

ErrorWhy it failsFix
Outputs not preservedFiles the model names are not uploaded, or an uploaded document is empty apart from its title.Upload every file the reply names, with its full content.
Browser address-bar linkPrivate to your session; reviewers cannot open it.Always use the product's Share feature, and save a link after every turn.
Deleting a chatKills every share link and fails the task.Never delete. Start a new chat per task.
Resubmit without addressing feedbackOnly the run or wording changed while the listed fixes stay open, so you get the same send-back.Work through the feedback item by item and tick each one off before you resubmit.
Reviewers cannot grade what they cannot open. A deleted chat, a private URL or a missing upload fails the task no matter how good the rest is.

Checklist
Before you submit

Re-read your task as a stranger

No matter how good the rest of the work is, these are the final checks.

The stranger re-read

  • Could a model finish it in one reply?
  • Is every name real in Gmail, Drive or Calendar?
  • Does each rubric line have one clear pass/fail answer?
  • Does anything depend on a draft being sent?

Hard-fails to double-check

  • An ungrounded entity: a file, person or date not in the universe
  • An LLM-written opening prompt
  • A deleted chat, or a browser address-bar URL as a link
  • A named file claimed in the reply with no matching upload
  • A PDF that contradicts the live share page
