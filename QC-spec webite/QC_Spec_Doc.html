Prompt
Unique Ground Truth
Rate the Unique Ground Truth of the Prompt dimension.
Score the ITEM as a whole: opening prompt + Target Outcomes + GTFA + Known / Not Known. Do not score the opening prompt on its own.
The opening prompt is REQUIRED to be abstract and partial (twelve rules #1 "partial request", #2 "start abstract", #7 "leave room for judgment", #10 "deliverables never in the opener"). Do NOT treat a vague opener, or missing details in the opener, as a defect here — that is the item working as designed.
The GTFA is not meant to be recoverable from the opening prompt. It is written from the Target Outcomes, which the model never sees. Withheld information is not the same as an undetermined answer.
What this dimension actually tests: once ALL Known / Not Known information has been supplied, does the item land on exactly one correct final state? For all options except the last, apply an error category.
2[Fail - No Unique Ground Truth] Even with all Known / Not Known information supplied, the item has more than one valid final answer. Two qualified experts working the same item would deliver different outputs and both would be correct. The correct outcome is subjective, requires an opinion, or is a matter of preference rather than fact. The GTFA does not state one correct final state: it is vague, it restates the deliverables without filling in the real values, or it is thin enough that nothing concrete can be graded against it.
3[Non-Fail - Minor Convergence Issues] The item resolves to a single main outcome, but a secondary element is left open — a non-critical value, an ordering, a formatting choice, or one unnamed side effect. Two strong runs would differ on that element without either being wrong. The GTFA states the correct final state but omits one of the four required areas (concrete deliverables / correct values / side effects / what must be absent), where the omission does not change what the right answer is.
5[No Issues] The item has a unique ground truth. The GTFA states one correct final state with the real values filled in, and covers all four areas: the concrete deliverables, the correct values and figures, the side effects, and what must be absent. Any remaining variance is equivalent-form only (e.g., 3.14 vs. π, IUPAC name vs. common name). Two contributors steering from the same item would land in the same place.

Deliverable Request
Rate the Deliverable Request of the Prompt dimension.
Score the ITEM: the opening prompt together with the Target Outcomes field. Do not score "the conversation" — in this project the contributor authors one opening prompt, not a multi-turn conversation. (The 1-3 turn simplified trajectory is a complexity test, not authored prompt content — do not score it here.)
Two things must be true:
1. The opening prompt contains a genuine ask. It may be loose and partial — 2-3 initial asks is the rule (twelve rules #1) — but the model must have something to act on in Turn 1. A pure statement of situation with no request at all is a defect.
2. The Target Outcomes require at least one CONCRETE produced output or landed side effect: a named artifact, a file, a draft, a calendar event, a connector action. Each entry must be verifiable against a produced artifact or a connector action.
Do NOT penalize the opener for withholding the full deliverable list. Rule #10 requires it: "Target outcomes go in a separate field. The full deliverable list lives in Target Outcomes — never in the opener." Nor for leading with motive rather than a task list (rule #5). Both are the item working as designed. For all options except the last, apply the error category.
2[Fail - No Deliverable Request] The Target Outcomes require nothing to be produced or done. The item resolves entirely to factual lookup, explanation or discussion, with no artifact, file, draft, event or connector action anywhere in the list.
2[Fail - No Deliverable Request] The outcomes are written as predicted user moves rather than final states (e.g., "the user might ask to fix the budget sheet"). A prediction cannot be graded - nobody can check whether a user "might" have asked for something.
2[Fail - No Deliverable Request] The Target Outcomes field is empty, or its entries are too unspecific to name what must be produced (e.g., "the model should give a good vendor recommendation").
2[Fail - No Deliverable Request] The opening prompt contains no ask of any kind: it states a situation and stops, giving the model nothing to act on in Turn 1.
 —
5[No Issues] The Target Deliverables require at least one concrete produced output or landed side effect, each entry verifiable against a produced artifact or a connector action, and each written as a final state rather than a predicted user move. The opening prompt contains a genuine, if partial, ask that gets the model started in Turn 1. Named artifacts carry the specific values and sections that must appear; named side effects carry recipients and timing.

Target Outcome
Rate the Target Outcome of the Prompt dimension.
The target outcome section lists the necessary components for a successful response. This can include components of artifacts and/or final model responses. If there are issues here, they likely indicate a misunderstanding of the prompt and may also be reflected in the rubric.
Note: The target outcome may include additional components beyond those required by the initial prompt in multiturn conversations. Check later prompts to ensure that a requirement established early on is not revised/removed/etc when checking the outcome list.
 —
3[Non-Fail - Misaligned Target Outcomes] The target outcomes list is misaligned with the prompt(s); it's incorrect, lists outcomes which are not necessary or expected by the prompt(s), or contradicts one or more prompts (except in cases where a requirement is put forth but is changed/removed in later prompts -- the target outcome should align with the expected final state).
5[No Issues] The target outcomes list is correct and fully aligned with the final state expected by the prompt(s).

Rubric Criteria
Weights
Rate the Weights of the Rubric Criteria dimension.
Note on weights: Criteria are weighted on a three-point scale: +1 (low), +2 (medium), +3 (high). "Off by one" means the assigned weight differs from the correct weight by 1 point (+1 vs +2, or +2 vs +3). "Off by two" means it differs by 2 points (+1 vs +3). For all options except the last, apply an error category.
2[Fail - Criteria Weights] At least 5% (inclusive) of the criteria weights are off by 2 points (+1 assigned where +3 was correct, or +3 where +1 was correct) At least 30% (inclusive) of the criteria weights are off by 1.
3[Non-Fail - Criteria Weights] Between 0 and 5% of the criteria weights are off by 2 points (+1 assigned where +3 was correct, or +3 where +1 was correct) Between 0 and 30% of the criteria weights are off by 1.
5[No Issues] All the criteria weights accurately reflect the impact of it on the response
