Six places tasks get sent back

Every rejection in Queue 1 traces to one of these six stages. Each deck slide takes one stage and lists the specific errors and the fix.

  1. 1

    Account & reset

    Right account, right model, reset before every run.

  2. 2

    Opening prompt

    2-3 loose asks, grounded, human-written, never one-shot.

  3. 3

    Deliverables & GTFA

    Name artifacts, values, side effects and absences, all traceable.

  4. 4

    Rubric criteria

    Binary, observable, atomic, positive, self-contained.

  5. 5

    Simplified run

    A fresh, unsteered 1-3 turn run that passes ≤ 40%.

  6. 6

    Submission & process

    Preserve outputs, use Share links, never delete a chat.

The single most common rejection is an ungrounded entity: a file, person or date not in the universe. Open Gmail, Drive and Calendar first and confirm every name on screen. Where rubrics fail: 30% unjudgeable wording · 26% punish the right answer, over half of all defects.

Before you submit

Re-read your own task as if you were a stranger seeing it for the first time. No matter how good the rest of the work is, the items on the right fail a task outright.

The stranger re-read

Four questions to ask yourself

  • Could a model finish it in one reply? If so, it is too easy.
  • Is every name real, something you can see in Gmail, Drive or Calendar?
  • Does each rubric line have one clear pass/fail answer?
  • Does anything depend on a draft being sent, which is never allowed?
Hard-fails to double-check

These fail a task outright

  • An ungrounded entity: a file, person or date not in the universe
  • An LLM-written opening prompt
  • A deleted chat: it kills every share link at once
  • A browser address-bar URL pasted instead of a Share link
  • A named file claimed in the reply with no matching upload
  • An uploaded PDF that contradicts the live share page
  • Turn 1 selected as the key turn (Queue 2)
  • Fewer than 7 turns in a Queue 2 run
  • An existing trajectory modified, re-run or deleted
  • Final trajectory link missing, or not a valid public share page
